# Roadmap: c6remote

Open work for c6remote, ordered by severity. Fabrication history, bring-up results and closed findings are in [`docs/timeline.md`](docs/timeline.md). The power budget is in [`docs/power-budget.md`](docs/power-budget.md).

## High

- [ ] **Measure the power budget on a rev-B board.** Everything in [`docs/power-budget.md`](docs/power-budget.md) is datasheet arithmetic: about 58 uA in deep sleep and about 31 mA awake. No figure is measured. The `led_vdd` and `ir_vdd` gates were both designed against that arithmetic and neither has been on a meter. Five rev-B remotes exist since 2026-08-24, so the measurement is now a bench task and not a fab-order gate. Rev-B is the `2026.8.0` board and carries no `Q3`, so `U2` stays powered there and the measured floor includes its 350 uA. Measure the deep-sleep current with `led_vdd` gated off, then compare it against the pre-gate 0.41 mA figure. The 58 uA figure needs a board that carries the `ir_vdd` gate, so it stays unmeasured until the next build. Before `Q3` the calculated floor was 0.41 mA, so the two gates are worth about 7x if the arithmetic holds. The `R7`/`R8` divider at 21 uA is the largest remaining static load and sits on the battery, so it cannot be switched.

- [ ] **Bench-verify the `ir_vdd` gate and the `TSOP6136` on the next build.** No built board carries `Q3`, `R11`, `ir_en` on `GPIO6`, the `TSOP6136` or the `Library:Vishay_PANHEAD-4Pin_TopView` land pattern, because rev-B is the `2026.8.0` board and predates both changes. Verify that the rail is off by default, that the `IR Rail` switch turns it on, and that `remote_receiver` decodes through the bottom face with the top-view lens. Also verify the datasheet pin order 1 GND / 2 N.C. / 3 Vs / 4 OUT against the land pattern.

## Medium

- [ ] **Add a reset button in the next board revision.** Connect a momentary button to the XIAO reset input. Place it where the enclosure permits access. Verify that a press restarts the ESP32-C6 without removing power.

- [ ] **Add the datasheet-required 100k pull-down on `sd` at the next board rework.** Raised 2026-08-24 while chasing low mic level. `DS-000069` page 12, "Data Output Mode": the `ICS-43434` `SD` pin tristates as soon as the LSB is output so another mic can drive a shared line, and "The SD trace should have a pull-down resistor to discharge the line during the time that all microphones on the bus have tristated their outputs. A 100 kOhm resistor is sufficient for this". The board has none: the `sd` net carries exactly two pads, `U1` pad 4 (`GPIO21`) and `MK1` pad 6, verified by walking every pad in `c6remote.kicad_pcb`. Batch it with the port widening below rather than spinning a board for it alone. Not the cause of the low level chased on 2026-08-24, and not urgent: with one mic on the bus the tristate window is the 24 unused bits plus the whole right half-frame, all of which land in the low 8 bits of the 32-bit slot that the firmware discards when it shifts right by 16, so a floating `SD` cannot reach the sample. It matters if a second mic is ever added, if the shift ever changes, or if the floating line couples enough to bias `GPIO21`. Cheapest interim check needs no board change: ESPHome cannot enable a pull-down on an I2S data pin through `i2s_audio`, so add a one-line `gpio_set_pull_mode(GPIO_NUM_21, GPIO_PULLDOWN_ONLY)` in `on_boot` after the mic starts and see whether the noise floor moves. Checked on the bench 2026-08-24 and it changed nothing, floor and speech peaks both 9 to 53 either way, which is what the paragraph above predicts; still worth fitting at the rework as datasheet compliance rather than as a fix.

- [ ] **Add `MK1`'s missing 0.1uF decoupling capacitor at the next board rework.** Raised 2026-08-25 while chasing low mic level. `MK1` has no local decoupling at all, and the board carries only three capacitors: `C1` 1uF on `+3.3V`, `C3` 100nF on `Net-(U2-Vs)`, `C4` 1uF on `led_vdd`. The nearest one on the mic's own rail is `C1`, **92.75mm away** beside `U1`; `C3` is 7.79mm from `MK1` but sits on the IR receiver rail, not `+3.3V`. So `MK1` pad 5 runs the full length of the board back to the LDO with nothing across it. `DS-000069` is unusually firm about this: the pin table calls for VDD "decoupled to GND with a 0.1 uF capacitor", and the Power Supply Decoupling section on page 15 says a "0.1 uF ceramic type X7R or better capacitor between Pin 5 (VDD) and ground is strongly recommended" to "avoid potential parasitic artifacts", with Figure 12 giving a recommended layout. Note the placement instruction is "as close to **Pin 3** as possible", pin 3 being GND, not pin 5 as you would assume; it is a return-path instruction and it is easy to get backwards. Batch it with the `sd` pull-down above and the port widening below.

  **Not the cause of the deaf mic**, and the distinction is worth stating because it is tempting. Missing decoupling raises the noise floor and, in the datasheet's own words, invites parasitic artifacts. It cannot subtract signal. What the bench actually shows is 21dB of missing signal, speech arriving at -56dBFS where -35dBFS is expected, and supply noise does not do that. Three things rule it out: the button-press transient reads -35dBFS through this same undecoupled supply, so the mic demonstrably delivers full level when something actually drives the diaphragm; sensitivity is specified flat across the whole 1.65 to 3.63V VDD range, so even real droop costs no dB; and the ICS-43434 is a digital part whose output is a fixed-point sample, not an analogue level that a soft rail can scale. What the missing cap probably does explain is the smaller anomaly nobody had accounted for: the measured idle floor sits near -80dBFS against a datasheet -90dBFS, and 10dB of excess noise is exactly what a 92mm unfiltered supply run into an undecoupled digital mic would buy. So fitting it should reclaim up to 10dB of SNR, which is real margin for STT, while leaving the acoustic deficit untouched.

- [ ] **Widen `MK1`'s acoustic port drill from 0.5mm to 0.8mm at the next board rework.** Raised 2026-08-24: the board can carry 0.8mm there safely, and a wider port is less acoustic resistance in front of a bottom-ported part, so take it rather than keeping the stock number by default. The port is the single NPTH in the stock `Sensor_Audio:InvenSense_ICS-43434-6_3.5x2.65mm` footprint, 0.5mm at footprint-local (0, -0.71), board (53.350, 31.582), on `*.Mask` only, and the mic hears through it from the bottom side (see `case/board.py` `npth_pads`, which is what the case's inlet is aimed at). One clearance number to settle before cutting the footprint: nearest copper is `MK1` pad 3 (GND, 0.3 x 0.3 SMD at local 0.6625, -0.71), whose edge sits 0.513mm from the port centre, so a 0.8mm hole leaves 0.113mm hole-edge to copper, under the 0.2 to 0.3mm NPTH-to-copper minimum fabs usually quote. Either confirm it with PCBWay or nudge the hole a tenth or two away from the pad 3 side. The stock library footprint cannot be edited in place, so this cuts a project-local copy into `kicad lib/Library.pretty/` and repoints `MK1` at it, same as the other custom footprints. Case side needs no parameter change: `case.mic_port_drill()` reads the drill off the board through `board.npth_pads`, so the funnel throat becomes the drill plus `MIC_THROAT_MARGIN` (1.3mm instead of 1.0mm) with `MIC_MOUTH_D` fixed at 3.0 and only `case.mic_fillet_r()` moving. After the board edit rerun `scripts/export-case-refs.sh` then `case/.venv/bin/python case/check.py`. Batch it with any other `MK1` rework rather than spinning a board for it alone.

- [ ] **Reconsider `MK1` itself, not just its port, at the next board rework.** Raised 2026-08-25 after the deaf-mic investigation moved its leading cause from acoustics to assembly. Three findings drive this, all in `docs/mic-v2-debugging.md`. First, the ICS-43434 has a public failure record matching this board exactly: two people on the ESPHome Discord built their own boards and got data, correct clocks, and no acoustic response, one across three PCBWay boards after a week of work, who then proved his ESP and pins good with an external INMP441 and gave up on the part. Both had the 0.1uF decoupling cap this board lacks, so that cap does not separate a working board from a broken one. Second, TDK forbids the cleaning steps a contract assembler runs by default: `AN-100` page 2 (now in `localdatasheets/`) says the package "can be damaged if subjected to cleaning processes. The cleaning solvents can enter through the port hole", plus no ultrasonic baths and no vapor phase soldering, and `DS-000069` page 18 adds no blow-off and no water at the port. Every one of those maps onto a step PCBWay describes in its own cleaning article, and PCBWay publishes no cleaning policy, no MSL or bake procedure and no reflow profile, so what this board actually got is unknown. Third, and this is the part that is our own design choice rather than the vendor's: mounting a bottom-port part on `B.Cu` with its port facing up into a 0.5mm NPTH creates a blind cavity, since the package seals the far end. Liquid entering from the top face cannot drain or air-dry, and dried residue leaves a permanent acoustic restriction. Widening the drill to 0.8mm per the item above makes that channel bigger, not safer.

  So the rework has three options, and the port-widening item alone settles none of them. Keep the ICS-43434 and carry the no-wash order item now in `docs/pre-fab-checklist.md`, which is the cheapest path but leaves the blind cavity. Or move to the `DMM-4026-B-I2S-R` already noted as the planned successor in the `TSOP6136` item above, confirming its EOL status and its own handling rules first, since every MEMS mic shares this fragility. Or change the mounting scheme rather than the part: a top-port mic on the outer face has no NPTH and no blind cavity, which removes the whole failure mode instead of managing it, at the cost of a case opening aligned to the package and a rethink of `case.mic_port_drill()`, which currently reads the drill off `board.npth_pads`. Decide the mounting question before the part question, because it changes which parts are candidates.

  **Confirmed per unit 2026-08-26, and this is now the strongest evidence in the item.** The build has five Rev B remotes. One mic works, one works with distortion, and three do not work, measured 2026-08-26. All five boards share one design, one firmware and one fab batch, so no design cause can produce that spread. The cause is a process or a handling step, which makes the no-wash order item in `docs/pre-fab-checklist.md` a requirement and not a precaution. This also confirms the `sd` pull-down and 0.1uF items above are real defects and not the cause. Every board carries both faults equally, so neither can separate a working unit from a dead one. The graded spread reproduces in-house what the PCBWay report describes, from excessive noise to no response. So the distorted unit is the same fault at a smaller dose. The known-good-breakout substitution test is retired. A working unit is a better positive control, it needs no wiring, and it is one board away.
- [ ] **Move the IR path under the board so both ends of it sit behind one aperture.** Raised 2026-08-17. `U2` (`TSOP6136`, `Library:Vishay_PANHEAD-4Pin_TopView`, B.Cu at 42.845,31.8575) and `D1` (`INL-3AHIR30`, `Library:LED_D3.0mm_Horizontal_O1.27mm_Z2.0mm_IRBlack`, B.Cu at 61.98,32.75 since 2026-08-17) both sit on the back face now, with `Q1` (`MMBT2222A`, 68.3125,32.3) and `R1` (470R, 69.0,35.75) beside `D1` on `IR EMIT`. `D1` is still a 3mm through-hole lamp, bent horizontal on a 2.0mm standoff. The shared aperture is what stays open: `case/README.md` records the lap joint at the IR end broken by both the IR relief and the emitter window, so the case still opens twice at that end. One shared window buys one less break in the skirt. The cost is an aiming decision, because a handheld remote is pointed by its nose, so an emitter that fires through the back face needs the case to redirect it or the user to tilt the remote. The cheaper variant of the same idea is to leave both firing out the +Y end wall and only share the aperture. Flipper data point, checked 2026-08-17: a Flipper Zero puts three transmitting IR LEDs and its receiver behind one IR-transparent window at the end of the case, so both directions share an aperture and neither fires through a face, and the receiver is the `TSOP75338`. Their emitter part number is not in the published documentation: `docs.flipper.net` serves `peripherals-schematic.pdf` through a presigned archbee URL that answers `{"status": "Not Allowed"}` to anything but a browser, so that sheet has to be opened by hand to read the LED line, and the Flipper blog gives only 940nm with no drive current. The SMD emitter swap was costed out and rejected on 2026-08-17, so `D1` stays through-hole; the side-view intensity figures behind that verdict are in [`docs/timeline.md`](docs/timeline.md). If the hand-formed lead is what needs to go, the cheap fixes are a lamp sold with pre-formed right-angle leads or a moulded LED holder that fixes height and angle, not a package change. Emitter drive follow-up for the same batch: `R3` went from 22R to 10R on 2026-08-17 for about 150mA, and `R1` stays at 470R for a forced beta of 28. Dropping `R1` to 220R gives 11.6mA of base for the last 15% and is still inside the C6's GPIO limits. Past roughly 300mA the base drive from a 20mA-capable GPIO becomes the limit on `Q1`'s Vce(sat), and that is the point where `Q1` wants to be a small N-FET with a reservoir cap on the emitter node, the same argument `C4` settled for the LED rail. Narrower is for range, not coverage, so if bring-up says coverage rather than reach, the second emitter to add is a wide one in parallel with its own resistor, never in series, because two Vf plus Vce(sat) leaves nothing across the resistor on 3.3V and the 0.8V to 1.5V Vf spread on that sheet would then set the current instead. Whoever picks this up: batch it with the `DMM-4026` mic swap rather than spinning a board for it, and rerun `scripts/export-case-refs.sh` then `case/.venv/bin/python case/check.py` afterwards, because both the bottom-side keepout heights and the window positions come out of the assembly STEP. Verify pad numbering and polarity against the datasheet before adopting any footprint, per the footprint rule in `AGENTS.md`.

## Low (cosmetic / config)

- [ ] **Add a dedicated Wi-Fi status LED in the next board revision.** Use steady colors for connected and disconnected states. Keep this indicator independent from the voice-assistant LEDs.

- [ ] **Fix silkscreen clipping and silk-over-copper.** `silk_edge_clearance` and `silk_over_copper` cases sit around the XIAO module. Move the reference designators inboard and off the pads. The artwork carries 24 `silk_over_copper`, 4 `silk_edge_clearance` and 1 `silk_overlap` case. The project ignores the first two rules, so they do not appear in the DRC count. 5 of the `silk_over_copper` cases are the ANO wheel outline crossing `ENC1` pads and are intentional.

## Back silkscreen artwork

Free-space survey of `B.SilkS` (pcbnew, 0.25mm raster, 0.35mm clearance from board edge, back-side mask openings, and existing back silk): the largest clear rectangle is **1930mm2 at x 35.39..71.64, y 85.51..138.76** (36.25 x 53.25mm), the lower-middle of the back. Second best is 462mm2 at x 35.39..71.64, y 38.26..51.01 (36.25 x 12.75mm) near the top. Back copper there is B.Cu `+3.3V` plane under soldermask, which does not trip `silk_over_copper` (that rule tests mask openings, not zones).

- [ ] **Radio capability marking. Use generic glyphs, not the certification logos.** Verdicts: **Bluetooth** word mark and figure mark are licensed only to Bluetooth SIG members whose product completed the Qualification Process. Espressif's ESP32-C6 QDID does not transfer, an end product needs its own Declaration ID, and while Adopter membership is free the Declaration fee is not (historically 4,000 to 8,000 USD). No hobbyist carve-out exists. **Wi-Fi** is the one favourable case: the April 2025 Wi-Fi Alliance Brand Style Guide has a "Brands for Public Use" tier that licenses the plain `Wi-Fi` word mark free to non-members, so the word is usable if spelled with the capitals and hyphen and not implying certification, but `Wi-Fi CERTIFIED` and the certification logo stay member-and-certification gated. **Zigbee** is the strictest: CSA's Nov 2025 guidelines require both Alliance membership and product certification for any brand or logo use, with no public tier. **Seeed logo** is out too, using it implies affiliation, though descriptive text naming the module is ordinary nominative use. Recommended unencumbered marking: a generic concentric-arc radio glyph plus `2.4 GHz`, optionally the bare IEEE standard numbers `802.11` and `802.15.4`, which are standard designators rather than certification marks. Fits the 36.25 x 12.75mm rectangle at y 38.26..51.01.
- [ ] **Module reference text.** Still open: a `B.SilkS` line naming the socket, `Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6`, plus board revision. The y 120..138 slot originally reserved for text is now the OSHW gear, so the remaining space is the y 38.26..51.01 rectangle at the top, shared with the radio marking above.
