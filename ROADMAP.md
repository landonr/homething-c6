# Roadmap: c6remote

Open work for c6remote, ordered by severity. Fabrication history, bring-up results and closed findings are in [`docs/timeline.md`](docs/timeline.md). The power budget is in [`docs/power-budget.md`](docs/power-budget.md).

## High

- [ ] **Measure the power budget on a rev-B board.** Everything in [`docs/power-budget.md`](docs/power-budget.md) is datasheet arithmetic: about 51 uA in deep sleep and about 31 mA awake. No figure is measured. The `led_vdd` and `ir_vdd` gates were both designed against that arithmetic and neither has been on a meter. Five rev-B remotes exist since 2026-08-24, so the measurement is now a bench task and not a fab-order gate. Rev-B is the `2026.8.0` board and carries no `Q3`, so `U2` stays powered there and the measured floor includes its 350 uA. Measure the deep-sleep current with `led_vdd` gated off, then compare it against the pre-gate 0.41 mA figure. The 58 uA figure needs a board that carries the `ir_vdd` gate, so it stays unmeasured until the next build. Before `Q3` the calculated floor was 0.41 mA, so the two gates are worth about 7x if the arithmetic holds. The `R7`/`R8` divider at 21 uA is the largest remaining static load and sits on the battery, so it cannot be switched.

- [ ] **Raise the microphone sample rate before the next build runs a `DMM-4026-B-I2S-R`.** Raised 2026-09-07 with the `MK1` swap. `c6remote.yaml` runs the mic at `sample_rate: 16000` with `bits_per_sample: 32bit`, which is a 1.024 MHz bit clock. The PUI data sheet page 1 gives an input clock of 2.048 to 4.096 MHz in normal mode and 320 kHz in sleep mode, so 1.024 MHz sits below the normal-mode minimum and in no defined mode. The ICS-43434 accepted 16 kHz, so this is new with the part and not a pre-existing defect. Use `sample_rate: 32000` for a 2.048 MHz clock or `sample_rate: 48000` for 3.072 MHz. Check what the change costs downstream first, because `on_data` in `c6remote.yaml` shifts each sample right by 16 and the voice assistant consumes the same stream. The bench cannot settle this until a board carries the new part, so it is a firmware change to make with the next build and not before.

- [ ] **Bench-verify the `ir_vdd` gate and the `TSOP6136` on the next build.** No built board carries `Q3`, `R11`, `ir_en` on `GPIO6`, the `TSOP6136` or the `Library:Vishay_PANHEAD-4Pin_TopView` land pattern, because rev-B is the `2026.8.0` board and predates both changes. Verify that the rail is off by default, that the `IR Rail` switch turns it on, and that `remote_receiver` decodes through the bottom face with the top-view lens. Also verify the datasheet pin order 1 GND / 2 N.C. / 3 Vs / 4 OUT against the land pattern.

## Medium

- [ ] **Add a reset button in the next board revision.** Connect a momentary button to the XIAO reset input. Place it where the enclosure permits access. Verify that a press restarts the ESP32-C6 without removing power.

- [ ] **Move the IR path under the board so both ends of it sit behind one aperture.** Raised 2026-08-17. `U2` (`TSOP6136`, `Library:Vishay_PANHEAD-4Pin_TopView`, B.Cu at 42.845,31.8575) and `D1` (`INL-3AHIR30`, `Library:LED_D3.0mm_Horizontal_O1.27mm_Z2.0mm_IRBlack`, B.Cu at 61.98,32.75 since 2026-08-17) both sit on the back face now, with `Q1` (`MMBT2222A`, 68.3125,32.3) and `R1` (470R, 69.0,35.75) beside `D1` on `IR EMIT`. `D1` is still a 3mm through-hole lamp, bent horizontal on a 2.0mm standoff. The shared aperture is what stays open: `case/README.md` records the lap joint at the IR end broken by both the IR relief and the emitter window, so the case still opens twice at that end. One shared window buys one less break in the skirt. The cost is an aiming decision, because a handheld remote is pointed by its nose, so an emitter that fires through the back face needs the case to redirect it or the user to tilt the remote. The cheaper variant of the same idea is to leave both firing out the +Y end wall and only share the aperture. Flipper data point, checked 2026-08-17: a Flipper Zero puts three transmitting IR LEDs and its receiver behind one IR-transparent window at the end of the case, so both directions share an aperture and neither fires through a face, and the receiver is the `TSOP75338`. Their emitter part number is not in the published documentation: `docs.flipper.net` serves `peripherals-schematic.pdf` through a presigned archbee URL that answers `{"status": "Not Allowed"}` to anything but a browser, so that sheet has to be opened by hand to read the LED line, and the Flipper blog gives only 940nm with no drive current. The SMD emitter swap was costed out and rejected on 2026-08-17, so `D1` stays through-hole; the side-view intensity figures behind that verdict are in [`docs/timeline.md`](docs/timeline.md). If the hand-formed lead is what needs to go, the cheap fixes are a lamp sold with pre-formed right-angle leads or a moulded LED holder that fixes height and angle, not a package change. Emitter drive follow-up for the same batch: `R3` went from 22R to 10R on 2026-08-17 for about 150mA, and `R1` stays at 470R for a forced beta of 28. Dropping `R1` to 220R gives 11.6mA of base for the last 15% and is still inside the C6's GPIO limits. Past roughly 300mA the base drive from a 20mA-capable GPIO becomes the limit on `Q1`'s Vce(sat), and that is the point where `Q1` wants to be a small N-FET with a reservoir cap on the emitter node, the same argument `C4` settled for the LED rail. Narrower is for range, not coverage, so if bring-up says coverage rather than reach, the second emitter to add is a wide one in parallel with its own resistor, never in series, because two Vf plus Vce(sat) leaves nothing across the resistor on 3.3V and the 0.8V to 1.5V Vf spread on that sheet would then set the current instead. Whoever picks this up: batch it with any other board change rather than spinning a board for it, because the `DMM-4026` mic swap it used to be batched with landed on 2026-09-07. Rerun `scripts/export-case-refs.sh` then `case/.venv/bin/python case/check.py` afterwards, because both the bottom-side keepout heights and the window positions come out of the assembly STEP. Verify pad numbering and polarity against the datasheet before adopting any footprint, per the footprint rule in `AGENTS.md`.

## Low (cosmetic / config)

- [ ] **Add a dedicated Wi-Fi status LED in the next board revision.** Use steady colors for connected and disconnected states. Keep this indicator independent from the voice-assistant LEDs.

- [ ] **Fix silkscreen clipping and silk-over-copper.** `silk_edge_clearance` and `silk_over_copper` cases sit around the XIAO module. Move the reference designators inboard and off the pads. The artwork carries 24 `silk_over_copper`, 4 `silk_edge_clearance` and 1 `silk_overlap` case. The project ignores the first two rules, so they do not appear in the DRC count. 5 of the `silk_over_copper` cases are the ANO wheel outline crossing `ENC1` pads and are intentional.

## Back silkscreen artwork

Free-space survey of `B.SilkS` (pcbnew, 0.25mm raster, 0.35mm clearance from board edge, back-side mask openings, and existing back silk): the largest clear rectangle is **1930mm2 at x 35.39..71.64, y 85.51..138.76** (36.25 x 53.25mm), the lower-middle of the back. Second best is 462mm2 at x 35.39..71.64, y 38.26..51.01 (36.25 x 12.75mm) near the top. Back copper there is B.Cu `+3.3V` plane under soldermask, which does not trip `silk_over_copper` (that rule tests mask openings, not zones).

- [ ] **Radio capability marking. Use generic glyphs, not the certification logos.** Verdicts: **Bluetooth** word mark and figure mark are licensed only to Bluetooth SIG members whose product completed the Qualification Process. Espressif's ESP32-C6 QDID does not transfer, an end product needs its own Declaration ID, and while Adopter membership is free the Declaration fee is not (historically 4,000 to 8,000 USD). No hobbyist carve-out exists. **Wi-Fi** is the one favourable case: the April 2025 Wi-Fi Alliance Brand Style Guide has a "Brands for Public Use" tier that licenses the plain `Wi-Fi` word mark free to non-members, so the word is usable if spelled with the capitals and hyphen and not implying certification, but `Wi-Fi CERTIFIED` and the certification logo stay member-and-certification gated. **Zigbee** is the strictest: CSA's Nov 2025 guidelines require both Alliance membership and product certification for any brand or logo use, with no public tier. **Seeed logo** is out too, using it implies affiliation, though descriptive text naming the module is ordinary nominative use. Recommended unencumbered marking: a generic concentric-arc radio glyph plus `2.4 GHz`, optionally the bare IEEE standard numbers `802.11` and `802.15.4`, which are standard designators rather than certification marks. Fits the 36.25 x 12.75mm rectangle at y 38.26..51.01.
- [ ] **Module reference text.** Still open: a `B.SilkS` line naming the socket, `Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6`, plus board revision. The y 120..138 slot originally reserved for text is now the OSHW gear, so the remaining space is the y 38.26..51.01 rectangle at the top, shared with the radio marking above.
- [ ] **Optional `homeThing` logo.** `docs/readme-assets/homeThingLogo.svg` is 3 paths on a near-square 805x795 viewBox, imports cleanly. Would compete with the flag for the same rectangle, so pick one as the hero.
- [ ] **After any further silk work:** rerun `pcb drc` and confirm that the `silk_edge_clearance`, `silk_over_copper` and `silk_overlap` counts do not rise above 4, 24 and 1. Then run `scripts/regen-fab.sh` so `export/` picks up the new silk. No zone refill is needed, because silkscreen does not touch copper.
