# Timeline and hardware revisions

This file is the fabrication, bring-up and design-change history of c6remote. Open work lives in [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md), and the power budget in [`docs/power-budget.md`](power-budget.md).

Two builds exist. Unit 1 is a single prototype from the [2026.6.0](https://github.com/landonr/homething-c6/releases/tag/2026.6.0) fab package, delivered by PCBWay on Monday 2026-07-20 (order YT1753739, PCB plus SMD assembly). The rev-B build is five assembled remotes, delivered on Monday 2026-08-24. The design has moved on since both, so read the comparison table below before you use a photo or a measurement from unit 1.

## Design decisions and closed findings

Every finding here is closed. The items are grouped by subsystem, not by date. Open work is in [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md).

### ENC1 rotary encoder

- **Pad 6/8 function swap, the root cause of a dead up, centre press and rotation.** Confirmed 2026-07-21 by a geometric match of `Library.pretty/Ano Rotary.kicad_mod` against Adafruit's own `ENCODER_ANO` Eagle footprint: all 9 pad radii match to 2 decimals, one 45 degree rotation, no mirror. Sources are learn.adafruit.com/ano-rotary-encoder/pinouts and github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-ANO-Rotary-Navigation-Encoder-Breakout-PCB. Pad 6, wired to `ano_sw2` on U3 P14, is physically COM_A, the shared return for centre push S1 and both encoder channels. Pad 8, wired to GND as a second common, is physically S4, a switch contact. COM_A floating on a GPIO net killed centre press and rotation, and the pad 8 switch was hard-tied to GND. It was a netlist fault, identical on both units, and a jumper from pad 6 to pad 1 (GND) restored centre press and rotation on unit 1 the same day.
- **Fix.** Symbol pins renamed to the datasheet functions (COM_A, COM_B, S1_CENTER, S2_DOWN, S3_RIGHT, S4_UP, S5_LEFT, ENCA, ENCB) with pin numbers 6 and 8 swapped, so the existing GND symbol and `ano_sw2` label land on the correct pads. The library copy and the embedded schematic copy were synced, which also cleared the old ERC symbol-mismatch item. Board pads resynced, the old pad 6 stub deleted and a new F.Cu route run from the `ano_sw2` trace at (52.0, 61.2) to pad 8. Verified 2026-07-26 in the board file, and proved on hardware 2026-08-25.
- **Tool warning from that fix.** The MCP `sync_schematic_to_board` also reassigned 5 pads and 4 segments to the virtual net PWR_FLAG, covering U1 BAT, MK1 VDD and the U2 Vs rail. All of it was hand-reverted. Do not trust that tool's net resolution near PWR_FLAG symbols.
- **The 90 degree rotation claim is retracted, 2026-07-27.** There is no rotation. Measured on unit 1 with `c6remote-test-v1.yaml`, reading actual expander edges per press: device-left fired P17, device-right fired P13, device-down fired P16 and device-centre fired P15. All four land on the `ENC1` symbol's own datasheet pin name, so the part is mounted as drawn. The two field observations behind the rotation claim were taken before the pad 6/8 fault was understood, on contacts that were dead. The corrected direction table is in `AGENTS.md`. It was applied to `c6remote.yaml` on 2026-07-27, and the test yamls already matched.
- **Channel names are crossed and it does not matter.** `ano_enc1` and `ano_enc2` land on ENCB and ENCA respectively, which only affects scroll count direction. Scroll direction was verified correct on unit 1 on 2026-07-27, so no `pin_a`/`pin_b` swap is needed in the yaml.
- **Encoder pull-ups and RC debounce, closed won't-do 2026-07-29.** Adafruit's own ANO Rotary Nav QT rev C (4/25/2023) fits no pull-up on either encoder channel or any of the five switches: `ENC_A`, `ENC_B` and `SW1`-`SW5` run straight to an ATtiny8x6 with `COM A` and `COM B` hard-grounded, and the internal pulls are set in firmware. Every resistor on that sheet is something else: R1/R2 10k are the SDA/SCL bus pull-ups, R3 10k is the UPDI pin, R4 and R7 10k are LED current limits, and R5G$1-4 10k are ADDR0-3 pull-downs behind solder jumpers SJ2-SJ5. There is no RC debounce on their switch lines either, which also kills the optional 10nF caps, because ESPHome debounces in software. Both channels already read correctly on unit 1 with nothing but the ESP32-C6 internal pull-up. What this board does have that Adafruit does not is length: `ano_enc1` is 91.81mm and `ano_enc2` is 73.79mm, both entirely on B.Cu with no vias, against a few mm on their module. That is a noise-pickup margin argument, not a speed one, because a 45k internal pull against tens of pF is microseconds and detents are tens of Hz. Revisit only if rev-B shows missed counts or phantom detents. The switch side needs nothing either: the PCF8575's quasi-bidirectional IO sources roughly 100uA when a bit is high, an effective 30k pull-up inside the part, which covers `sw1`-`sw11` and `ano_sw1`-`ano_sw5`.

### Discrete switches

- **TL3315 terminal mapping short.** First prototype bring-up on 2026-07-20 confirmed PCF8575 communication, but SW1-8 and SW10-11 read permanently pressed while all five ANO encoder switches read released. TL3315NF160Q terminals 1 and 2 are internally common, as are terminals 3 and 4, and the footprint put GND on pad 1 and `sw*` on pad 2, which shorted every populated switch input to GND. SW9 read released, most likely from an open or incomplete joint, which does not invalidate the mapping fault.
- **Fix.** Schematic done 2026-07-21: the generic 2-pin `Switch:SW_Push` was replaced with a 4-pin `Local:SW_TL3315NF160Q` in a new `Local.kicad_sym` registered in `sym-lib-table`, pins 1 and 2 stacked on the GND node and pins 3 and 4 on the `sw*` node. Board copper remapped and verified 2026-07-26: all 11 switches read pads 1 and 2 on GND and pads 3 and 4 on `sw<n>`, checked individually. Proved on hardware 2026-08-25.
- **Footprint and 3D model, resolved 2026-07-17.** PCBWay confirmed by email (Freya, 2026-07-16) that they bought the exact TL3315NF160Q from the BOM, and the assembly photo shows the matching SMD part, so the footprint and the BOM were already correct. The 3D model was the wrong part entirely: `SW_SPST_PTS647Sx50_black.step`, a 5.0mm tall plunger, replaced with a generated `3dmodels/TL3315NF160Q.wrl` built from the TL3315 datasheet drawing (4.5x4.5x0.55 ultra low profile, dark gray housing, 4.0 dia gold snap dome, silver gull wings at 4.75 span). The manufacturer STEP was rejected as a flat uncolored slab with no dome. WRL renders in KiCad but is excluded from board STEP exports, which is acceptable because 0.55mm is negligible for enclosure work.

### Microphone

- **MK1 ICS-43434 pin numbering fault, the root cause of the dead microphone on unit 1.** Found 2026-07-27 on the first hardware exercise of the mic. MK1 reused the `inmp441_microphone_i2s:INMP441_Microphone_I2S` symbol, whose pin numbers are the INMP441's (1 SCK, 2 WS, 3 L/R, 4 SD, 5 VDD, 6 GND) and not the ICS-43434's (1 WS, 2 LR, 3 GND, 4 SCK, 5 VDD, 6 SD, per DS-000069 v1.2 Table 8). On the fabbed board that put the 3.072MHz bit clock on the mic WS pin, the 48kHz frame clock on LR, left the real SCK pin floating on the undriven `sd` net, and soldered the SD output pad into the GND pour. The symptom was 100% zero samples at the correct 192000 B/s byte rate on both `channel:` settings, because a mic clocked under 200kHz sits in standby with SD tristated while the ESP samples its own high-impedance clock input. Nets and footprint were both innocent: the stock `Sensor_Audio:InvenSense_ICS-43434-6_3.5x2.65mm` has pad N equal to physical pin N, checked against datasheet Fig 3 and Fig 15. This was the third instance of the pad-numbering class after the ENC1 pad 6/8 swap and the SK6812 symbol and footprint pairing, and the specific trap is reusing a symbol named for a different part in the same family.
- **Fix, 2026-07-27 at `b2d85c0`.** Pins renumbered in both the embedded copy and the project-local `inmp441_microphone_i2s.kicad_sym`, with positions and electrical types untouched, so every wire endpoint and net-to-function pairing survived. The board was resynced with MK1 rotated 180 to 0 in place and the mic cluster rerouted. That reroute also swapped the two signals at the XIAO end, `ws` moving to GPIO0 and `sd` to GPIO21, which every firmware then contradicted until `634d967`. Left uncorrected that is worse than the original fault, because driving LRCLK on GPIO21 puts a push-pull ESP output on the mic's SD output pad. The rule this bought: check firmware against the exported netlist, never against another firmware, which is how the swap survived a pin-by-pin review.
- The full bring-up log was `docs/mic-bringup.md`. It was deleted 2026-07-31 and is recoverable at `7b3563c`.
- **Unit 1 is not recoverable by rework**, unlike the ENC1 and SW1-11 faults. The mic SD pad is tied into the GND pour with no path to any GPIO, and the part is a bottom-terminal LGA, so nothing can be jumpered to it. `c6remote-test-mic.yaml` keeps reporting zeros on that build.
- The rev-B microphone investigation is still open. It lives in `docs/mic-v2-debugging.md`, with the board-side rework items in `ROADMAP.md`.

### Status LEDs and the `led_vdd` rail

- **D2 supply rail decided.** `VCC`, meaning `U1`/14 VBUS, would have left the LED dead on battery, so D2/4 and C2/1 were rewired to `+3.3V`.
- **D2 runs below its datasheet VDD, found 2026-07-25, accepted 2026-07-26.** `SK68XX MINI` rev 08 specs VDD 3.7-5.5V and `WS2812B-2020` V1.3 specs 3.7-5.3V, so both the old and the new status LED sat under spec on the 3.3V rail. Data input was unaffected, because the `WS2812B-2020` VIH minimum is an absolute 2.7V. The expected symptom was dim or colour-shifted blue (blue Vf 2.7-3.0V), not failure, and the prototype ran an SK6812MINI at 3.3V fine. Capping brightness does not help, because WS2812 parts dim by PWM duty at constant current, so the mitigation is colour choice. Closed on hardware 2026-08-25 when all four LEDs lit.
- **All four rail alternatives were priced out 2026-07-26 and rejected.** `VCC` (`U1`/14 `VBUS_14`, 4.75-5.25V) is zero on battery. `BAT` (`U1`/32, unregulated 3.0-4.2V) is in spec only for the top third of a LiPo curve, worse than the LDO at 3.0-3.3V, and it drifts LED colour across a charge cycle. A 0.3V Schottky diode-OR of VBUS and BAT leaves BAT at 3.4V, still under, because the drop eats the headroom where it is needed. A 5V boost is in spec in every state but costs board area, runs continuously, and has no pin left to enable it. The regulated 3V3 LDO output stays. If this is revisited, swap the part and not the rail.
- **The 3.3V-rated 2020 LED search, 2026-07-26.** Exactly one 2020 addressable RGB is datasheet-rated to 3.3V: Dialight `587-1024-147F` (3.3-5.5V, 2.0x2.0x0.9mm, single wire, 5mA, POR 2.6V), corner-for-corner identical to the `WS2812B-2020` so copper would not move, with only the pad numbering differing (VDD pin 2, GND pin 4). It was rejected on sourcing and not on merit: LCSC `C22471867` stock 0, DigiKey 12,743 at $0.85/1 and $0.339/1000 with a 15-week lead, 5 to 7 times the Chinese parts. Also checked and rejected: `WS2812C-2020-V1` (`C2976072`, 481k stock, 3.7-5.3V, VIH 0.7*VDD, no gain), `SK6812-2020` and `EC20-6812` (characterized only at 4.5-5.5V, zero LCSC results), `TX1812Z5` (stock 0 and 30, 3.3V only empirically) and `IN-PI554FCH` (`C22219545` stock 0, a 5050 at 25mm2, out on size). No 3535 was pursued once a 2020 candidate existed. The physical reason nothing is rated below 3.5V: blue InGaN Vf is 2.8-3.0V at 5-12mA and the constant-current sink needs headroom above that, so at 3.3V the sink is in dropout and only a boost changes that.
- **Swap to `XL-2020RGBC-WS2812B`, 2026-07-26.** XINGLIGHT, LCSC `C5349955`, 183,977 stock, $0.101/1 to $0.060/500, cheaper than the `WS2812B-2020` it replaces, whose LCSC line `C965555` is flagged discontinued anyway (stock 1, successor `C52917434`). Gains: VDD 3.5-5.5V, VIH 0.5*VDD instead of an absolute 2.7V, and 5mA per channel instead of 12mA, which is the one that matters because blue Vf falls with current. Cost: roughly 3 times dimmer (B 50-150 against 100-200 mcd), acceptable for state display. Protocol unchanged, so ESPHome keeps `chipset: WS2812`, `rgb_order: GRB` and `num_leds: 4`. Pin numbering is identical (1 DO, 2 GND, 3 DI, 4 VDD) per the DigiKey-hosted revision, so no net remap was needed.
- **Land pattern cut by hand for that swap.** The part is a 2.0x2.0mm body wanting 0.85x0.75mm pads at plus and minus 0.675 X and 0.625 Y, where the stock 2.2x2.0 footprint had 0.7x0.7 pads at plus and minus 0.915 and 0.55, so the new terminals overlapped only about 59% of the old pads and were biased outboard. The new `kicad lib/Library.pretty/LED_XL-2020RGBC-WS2812B_PLCC4_2.0x2.0mm.kicad_mod` uses the datasheet pads, silk at plus and minus 1.3 and 1.2, courtyard at plus and minus 1.35 and 1.25, and a 2.0x2.0 F.Fab body. Positions, rotations, uuids and nets were all preserved and every track stub still lands inside its new pad. One catch: the smaller pads starved D5 pad 2's thermal relief to 1 spoke against a minimum of 2, so all four VSS pads now carry `zone_connect 2`, a solid pour connection with lower return inductance.
- **LED placement.** All four sit on a square around the ANO wheel, radius 19.046273mm from the centre at (53.337455, 67.714045), at 45, 135, 225 and 315 degrees, so D2 is bottom-left, D3 top-left, D4 top-right and D5 bottom-right, 1.85mm outside the 17.2mm wheel outline so the chain reads as a ring. Rotations are D2 -45, D3 -135, D4 135 and D5 -135. Gotcha for pad maths read out of the board file: KiCad applies footprint rotation as `-theta` in y-down board coordinates, so a naive `+theta` transform gives plausible but wrong pad positions on any footprint that is not at 0 or 180.
- **Block shrunk from 3 parts to 1.** R2 330R was deleted, because 330R is a current-limit value and not the 22-100R data-line damping value, and roughly 5mm of trace needs neither, so `GPIO17` drives DIN directly. C2 1uF was deleted. The old sourcing line was `Adafruit / MPN 4960`, a 10-pack SKU and not a reel.
- **Chain extended to 4 LEDs.** D3-D5 use the same part, footprint and sourcing as D2, cascaded off D2 DOUT, with D5 DOUT left no-connect. One GPIO and three new nets, no existing net touched. Peak is 60mA full white at 5mA per channel on the 700mA XIAO 3V3 rail.
- **LED chain decoupling, decided twice.** On 2026-07-26 the answer was no caps at all, because every VDD pad dropped into the board-wide B.Cu `+3.3V` plane through a via 0.00mm (D3, on-pad), 1.17mm (D5), 1.92mm (D4) and 4.41mm (D2) away, so each supply loop was a few nH. Two paper arguments were wrong along the way: distance to C2 never mattered because C2 was not in the current path, and a cap cannot help a DC shortfall. Plane-pair capacitance is not the mechanism either, because 2500mm2 over 1.6mm FR4 is only about 60pF. **Reopened 2026-07-28**, because the rail gate deleted the premise: `led_vdd` is now a trace tree of roughly 90mm with 4 vias plus the FET channel between the plane and the LEDs, so the LEDs no longer sit on a plane at all. Re-decided the same day as one bulk cap. `C4` is 1uF 50V X7R 0805, Yageo `CC0805KKX7R9BB105`, deliberately the same MPN as C1 so the BOM groups them into one line, and 50V X7R means negligible DC bias derating at 3.3V unlike a 6.3V X5R. It sits on B.Cu at (46.75, 67.3), mid-chain rather than beside Q2, because the point is local charge at the LEDs. Its ground return is a 5.2mm B.Cu stub into `ENC1` pad 6, a through-hole GND pad.
- **`led_vdd` gate detail, 2026-07-28.** `Q2` is an `AO3401A` P-channel MOSFET in SOT-23 (Alpha and Omega, Digi-Key 1855773, LCSC `C15127`), source on `+3.3V`, drain on `led_vdd`, gate on `led_en`. Rds(on) is about 60 milliohm at Vgs -2.5V, so 22mV of drop at the 60mA full-white peak. `R10` is a 1M 0603 (Yageo `RC0603FR-071ML`) gate pull-up to `+3.3V`, which makes the rail default off at a cost of 3.3uA while the rail is on. `led_en` lands on `U1` pad 11, `GPIO18`/D10. The four `+3.3V` vias that used to drop each LED VDD pad into the B.Cu plane were removed, and `led_vdd` routes 39.3mm on F.Cu plus 51.9mm on B.Cu with 4 vias. **Layout trap:** the first routing attempt ran `led_vdd` diagonally across F.Cu past the wheel, severed the GND pour neck and orphaned D5's VSS pad in a 77mm2 island, which produced the only unconnected item DRC found. Moving that branch to B.Cu fixed it and left the pour healthier than before.
- **`led_vdd` firmware, 2026-07-29**, in `c6remote.yaml` and `c6remote-test-common.yaml`, because `GPIO17` must be low or high-impedance whenever the rail is down. Four pieces: the `LED Rail` switch is `platform: gpio` on `GPIO18` with `inverted: true` and `restore_mode: ALWAYS_OFF`; the light's `on_turn_on` raises the rail, waits 20ms and re-sends, because the first frame is written in the same loop tick as the trigger; its `on_turn_off` waits 100ms so the final all-black frame lands while the LEDs still have power; and `on_shutdown` drops the rail and puts both pins back to inputs, `GPIO17` with a pulldown. Both turn-off paths are guarded on light state. An `on_turn_off` interlock on the switch itself blanks the light first, because the switch is exposed as a `config` entity. One residual is accepted: the strip component writes one all-black frame at setup before any automation runs, so DIN sees roughly 96 bit-symbols while the rail is still down.

### IR receiver and the `ir_vdd` rail

- **Supply filter added.** `R6` 100R 0603 inline into `U2`/3 and `C3` 100nF 0603 between `U2`/3 and `U2`/2, placed at 38.5,33 and 40.5,35.5.
- **`TSOP75336` was the candidate and was rejected, 2026-08-17.** It was picked on package size alone and would have changed the AGC class. Vishay doc 80076 gives the third digit of the part number as the AGC setting: `TSOP4136` is AGC1, "most IR codes, short int", the most permissive setting Vishay ships. The Heimdall package has no AGC1 part at all, because doc 82495 offers only AGC3 and AGC5 and doc 82494 only AGC2 and AGC4, so `TSOP75336` is AGC3. That is the wrong direction for this board, because both `c6remote.yaml` and `c6remote-test-common.yaml` run `remote_receiver` with `dump: all`, so a code the AGC rejects as noise can never be learned. `TSOP6136` (Panhead, doc 82457) is AGC1 at 36kHz in an SMD package, so decode behaviour is untouched. The price is range, 18m typ against 24m, and `Ee min` for RC5 of 0.20 against 0.12 mW/m2, neither of which matters across a living room. Height cost nothing either, because Panhead's 5.3mm and Heimdall's 3.0mm are both far under the 10.43mm the through-hole part stood. `ISD` is 0.35mA typ on both. Sourced as `TSOP6136TT`, Digikey 4075852.
- **Three parts had to be cut for that swap**, because KiCad stock carries no Panhead land pattern and every stock `Interface_Optical` TSOP symbol is 3-pin: `kicad lib/Library.pretty/Vishay_PANHEAD-4Pin_SideView.kicad_mod` (four 0.9 x 2.2 pads on 1.27 pitch per doc 82457 page 7), the symbol `TSOP61xx` in `c6remote-kicad/Local.kicad_sym`, and `c6remote-kicad/3dmodels/TSOP6136.step` from doc 82826, re-exported pre-rotated so the footprint needs no offset or rotate. Pinning moved from 1 OUT, 2 GND, 3 Vs to 1 GND, 2 N.C., 3 Vs, 4 OUT, but the new symbol keeps OUT, GND and Vs on the old pin coordinates, so no wire endpoint moved and only the numbers changed. Pad 2 carries a no-connect flag, and pad 1 needs a via into the F.Cu GND pour, because B.Cu is the `+3.3V` plane and an SMD pad cannot reach the pour through a barrel the way the old through-hole pad did. All 171 board pad nets were diffed against the schematic netlist for zero mismatches after the F8 sync.
- **The TT and TR record was wrong and was corrected 2026-08-21.** The swap commit recorded TT and TR as tape attitudes sharing one mounting orientation. They are lead forms: TT receives perpendicular to the board and TR receives parallel to it. Both share the one land pattern, and the BOM had carried the TT MPN since `e6da487`, so only the footprint disagreed with the ordered part.
- **`ir_vdd` gate, 2026-08-04.** `U2` was 86% of the deep-sleep current on its own at ISD 0.35mA typ and 0.45mA max, rising to 0.45mA typ under 40klx sunlight. The open question was whether IR receive is meant to be a deep-sleep wake source, which would force `U2` to stay powered. It is not and cannot be as wired, because `IR REC` sits on `GPIO16` and the C6 wakes only on `GPIO0`-`GPIO7`. `Q3` is a second `AO3401A` with the same part, footprint and sourcing as `Q2`, and `R11` is a 1M 0603 gate pull-up. `R6`'s upper end moved from `+3.3V` to `ir_vdd`, so the gate sits upstream of the existing `R6`/`C3` filter. `ir_en` lands on `U1` pad 29, `GPIO6`, which had carried a no-connect flag, leaving `GPIO7` (pad 25) as the only spare. Placement follows the gate and not the load, which is the opposite of `Q2`: the whole `ir_en` net is compact down by `U1` because it is a 1M high-impedance node, while `ir_vdd` takes the 110mm run up to `R6`, where 350uA over that length is about 0.5 ohm and 0.2mV. **Layout trap, the same one `led_vdd` hit:** `Q3` and `R11` were first placed on F.Cu with `ir_en` routed down the left side past `U1`, which pinched the F.Cu GND pour into pieces that could not reach each other. DRC reported it as one `isolated_copper` warning plus `unconnected_items` whose two items were both `Zone [GND] on F.Cu`, which is the signature to watch for. Note that the JSON export reports the zone anchor and not the island, so every zone violation looks like it is at the top-left corner. Fixed with stitching vias.
- **`ir_vdd` firmware, 2026-08-04.** An `IR Rail` gpio switch on `GPIO6`, `inverted: true`, but `restore_mode: ALWAYS_ON` rather than the LED rail's `ALWAYS_OFF`, because `remote_receiver` cannot decode with `U2` unpowered. Turning it off while awake is not useful, because `GPIO16` carries a pullup and `U2` has an internal 30k from OUT to Vs, which leaks roughly 44uA back into a dead rail, so the saving exists only in deep sleep where the pullup is released.

### IR emitter

- **Current-limit resistor added.** The original design had no series R between `+3.3V` and `D1`, only the base R1 470R on `Q1`, which would have destroyed the IR LED or the transistor. `R3` 22R 0603 was added in series to the D1 anode, placed at (55, 33.258) on F.Cu with a via to the B.Cu `+3.3V` pour. It went to 10R on 2026-08-17.
- **The SMD emitter swap was costed out and rejected, 2026-08-17, so `D1` stays through-hole.** The emitter has to fire out the top end wall, the same end the receiver looks out of, because that is the end a remote gets pointed with, so an SMD part would have to be a side-view package, and side-view is where IR emitter selection falls apart. Vishay's side-looking `VSMx14940` family runs 20mW/sr to 35mW/sr typ at 100mA in a 2.3 x 2.55 x 2.3mm package, `VSMB2943SLX01` is 15mW/sr at 25 degrees, and `VSMB10940` is 150 degrees wide, against roughly 50mW/sr for the `INL-3AHIR30` at the same 100mA, scaled from its 10mW/sr at 20mA. The 3mm lamp's moulded lens is what buys that, and no side-view chip package in this size class has one. The high-intensity SMD parts, Vishay `VSMY2940GX01` at 145mW/sr at 100mA with a 10 degree half angle and ams-osram `SFH 4441` at 65mW/sr at 34 degrees, are all top-emitting, so using one means either a 45 degree mirror or light pipe in the case, or giving up on emitting along the remote's axis. Those side-view figures are distributor parametric data and are not datasheet-verified, because Vishay ships those sheets with outlined text that will not extract. So the SMD win on this board is the receiver only.

### Expander

- **I2C pull-ups added.** `R4` 4.7k on SDA and `R5` 4.7k on SCL, both to `+3.3V`, placed on F.Cu near `U1` and `U3`. The ESP32-C6 internal pulls are too weak for a PCF8575 with 16 keys and a rotary.
- **PCF8575 INT handling, corrected 2026-07-27.** The line is wired as `exp_int` to XIAO pin 24 (`GPIO5`) with `R9` 100k pull-up to `+3.3V`. The hardware path exists, but "interrupt-driven" overstated it: ESPHome's `pcf8574` component polls the expander over I2C and has no hook for an external interrupt pin, so nothing in firmware reacts to this line and button latency is set by the poll loop. What it actually buys is observability, because an edge appears whenever any expander input changes even when the port bit itself is misrouted, which is how the dead ANO up direction was proven a hardware fault rather than a mis-mapped bit. It is declared as a diagnostic binary sensor in the bring-up firmwares and in `c6remote.yaml` at `758581b`. Making it genuinely interrupt-driven needs a custom component or an upstream change, not a config edit. The internal pullup in both configs is redundant against `R9` but harmless.
- **U3 decoupling declined, 2026-07-17.** `C1` (1uF 0805) already sits about 2mm from `U3`/24 on F.Cu with a short loop, and the PCF8575 is a slow I2C GPIO expander (400kHz bus, static outputs) with negligible HF supply demand. A 100nF would only help at HF, and the F.Cu corner around pin 24 is fully routed for the SDA/SCL fanout to `R4` and `R5`, so the only free spot was B.Cu through vias, whose inductance negates the low-ESL benefit. The prototype `C4` addition was reverted, and that designator was later reused for the `led_vdd` bulk cap, so a `C4` reference anywhere after 2026-07-28 means the LED rail cap and not a U3 part.
- PCF8575 communication itself was verified during the first prototype bring-up on 2026-07-20.

### Mounting

- The three `H1`-`H3` instances are `board_only`, `exclude_from_pos_files` and `exclude_from_bom`, which keeps parity at 0 without tripping `lib_footprint_mismatch`, and leaves the position file at 37 rows and the BOM untouched. The footprint is a single 2.4mm `np_thru_hole` pad with no annular ring, a 2.2mm courtyard, a hidden reference so it adds no silk, and a `Cmts.User` circle at the head diameter so the keepout is visible in the editor. After the M2 change the tightest margin went from +0.112mm to +0.612mm, where `ws` runs 2.612mm from H1's centre on B.Cu, the face the screw heads bear on, with H2 at +0.900 to `ir_vdd` and H3 at +1.070 to `sw10`.
