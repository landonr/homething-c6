- [x] **MK1 ICS-43434 pin numbering fault (root cause of the dead microphone).** Found 2026-07-27 on the first hardware exercise of the mic, full bring-up log in `docs/mic-bringup.md`. MK1 reused the `inmp441_microphone_i2s:INMP441_Microphone_I2S` symbol, whose pin numbers are the INMP441's (1 SCK, 2 WS, 3 L/R, 4 SD, 5 VDD, 6 GND) and not the ICS-43434's (1 WS, 2 LR, 3 GND, 4 SCK, 5 VDD, 6 SD, per DS-000069 v1.2 Table 8). On the fabbed board that put the 3.072MHz bit clock on the mic WS pin, the 48kHz frame clock on LR, left the real SCK pin floating on the undriven `sd` net, and soldered the SD output pad into the GND pour. Symptom was 100% zero samples at the correct 192000 B/s byte rate on both `channel:` settings, because a mic clocked under 200kHz sits in standby with SD tristated while the ESP samples its own high-Z clock input. Nets and footprint were both innocent: the stock `Sensor_Audio:InvenSense_ICS-43434-6_3.5x2.65mm` has pad N equal to physical pin N, checked against datasheet Fig 3 and Fig 15. Third instance of this class after the ENC1 pad 6/8 swap and the SK6812 symbol/footprint pairing, so the `AGENTS.md` rule about checking pad numbering against the datasheet before adopting a part now has three scalps, and the specific trap here is reusing a symbol named for a different part in the same family. Resolved on schematic and board 2026-07-27 at b2d85c0: pins renumbered in both the embedded copy and the project-local `inmp441_microphone_i2s.kicad_sym`, positions and electrical types untouched so every wire endpoint and net-to-function pairing survived; board resynced with MK1 rotated 180 to 0 in place and the mic cluster rerouted, pads now `ws` on 1, GND on 2 and 3, `sck` on 4, `+3.3V` on 5, `sd` on 6, tracks 289 to 319 segments. The reroute also swapped the two signals at the XIAO end, `ws` moving to GPIO0 and `sd` to GPIO21, which every firmware then contradicted until 634d967; left uncorrected that is worse than the original fault, since driving LRCLK on GPIO21 puts a push-pull ESP output on the mic's SD output pad instead of merely leaving the part in standby. Current wiring is tabulated in `docs/mic-bringup.md`; check firmware against the exported netlist, never against another firmware, which is how the swap survived a pin-by-pin review. ## High

- [ ] **Gate the LED rail: D2-D5 burn ~2.4mA quiescent even when dark.** Raised 2026-07-27. The four `XL-2020RGBC-WS2812B` sit permanently on `+3.3V`, and a WS2812-class part keeps its controller die powered and listening whenever VDD is present, so writing all channels to zero only zeroes the PWM duty and does not reduce the standing draw. At roughly 0.6mA each that is ~2.4mA continuous, already flagged in the LED chain item below as the dominant idle load, and it swamps everything else on the board: the R7/R8 battery divider is ~21uA, the PCF8575 a few uA, and a sleeping ESP32-C6 tens of uA. Consequence for power work: deep sleep cannot beat ~2.4mA no matter how well it is implemented, so this is the binding constraint on battery life and it caps the value of the wake-on-nINT item under Medium. Fix is a rev-B board change, a high-side PFET or load switch on the LED `+3.3V` feed driven by a spare GPIO. Pins are available despite the old "no spare pins" claim in `AGENTS.md`, now corrected: `GPIO18` (U1 pad 11, D10, already broken out to `TP_GPIO18_D10_MOSI`), plus `GPIO6` (pad 29) and `GPIO7` (pad 25) on the bottom row, whose routability is proven by `bat_sense` and `exp_int` already coming off `GPIO4` and `GPIO5` there. Three constraints when implementing. Keep it high-side: all four LED VSS pads are `zone_connect 2` solid into the F.Cu GND pour, so low-side switching would mean cutting them out of the plane. Drive `GPIO17` low or high-impedance whenever the rail is down, otherwise DIN pushes current through the LED input clamp into the dead rail and partially powers the chain, defeating the point. And pick a switch whose own quiescent is negligible against 2.4mA, which most load switches are at about 1uA. Payoff is idle dropping from ~2.4mA to the tens of uA, the difference between days and months on a battery.

## Medium

- [x] **Decide PCF8575 INT (U3/1) handling.** Wired to net `exp_int` → XIAO pin24 (GPIO5), with R9 100k pull-up to +3.3V. The hardware path exists, but "interrupt-driven" overstated it and is corrected here 2026-07-27: ESPHome's `pcf8574` component polls the expander over I2C and has no hook for an external interrupt pin, so nothing in firmware reacts to this line and button latency is set by the poll loop, not by nINT. What it actually buys is observability, an edge appears on it whenever any expander input changes even when the port bit itself is misrouted, which is how the dead ANO up direction was proven a hardware fault rather than a mis-mapped bit. Declared as a diagnostic binary sensor in the bring-up firmwares since bring-up and in `c6remote.yaml` at 758581b. Making it genuinely interrupt-driven would need a custom component or an upstream change, not a config edit; the internal pullup in both configs is redundant against R9 but harmless.
- [x] **Add 100nF decoupling at U3 PCF8575 VDD.** Evaluated 2026-07-17, decided NOT to add. C1 (1µF 0805) already sits ~2mm from U3/24 on F.Cu with a short loop. PCF8575 is a slow I2C GPIO expander (400kHz bus, static outputs) with negligible HF supply demand, so C1 alone is sufficient. A 100nF would only help at HF, and the F.Cu corner around pin24 is fully routed (SDA/SCL fanout to R4/R5), so the only free spot was B.Cu-through-vias, whose via inductance negates the low-ESL HF benefit. Not worth the redundancy. Prototype C4 addition was reverted.
- [x] **Shrink and simplify the D2 status LED block.** Was 3 parts (SK6812MINI 3535 12.25mm² + R2 330Ω series on `led_1` + C2 1uF 0805), now 1: a 2020 PLCC4 LED alone, 4.0mm² for the size win (final part `XL-2020RGBC-WS2812B`, see the swap item under High; a `WS2812B-2020` stopgap lived one day in between). R2 deleted: 330Ω is a current-limit value, not the 22-100Ω data-line damping value, and ~5mm of trace needs none, GPIO17 drives DIN directly. C2 deleted: the layout feeds the LEDs off the planes (see the LED chain decoupling item below). Sourcing fixed too: old line was `Adafruit / MPN 4960`, a 10-pack SKU not a reel. ESPHome `chipset:` SK6812 to WS2812. Schematic verified 2026-07-25: ERC 0, netlist delta exactly R2 gone and `Net-(D2-DIN)` merged into `led_1` (43 to 41 nets). Board done 2026-07-26: footprint swapped, R2 pads and stubs gone, `led_1` rerouted to DIN, zones refilled, DRC 0 errors.
- [x] **Extend status light to a 4-LED chain.** Added D3-D5 (same part, footprint, sourcing as D2) cascaded off D2: `GPIO17` → `led_1` → D2 DIN, then DOUT → `led_2`/`led_3`/`led_4` → next DIN, D5 DOUT no-connect. - [x] **Decide LED chain decoupling.** Decided 2026-07-26: NO caps at all, C2 (the last one) deleted, because the layout already feeds each LED off the planes. Every VDD pad drops into the board-wide B.Cu `+3.3V` plane through a via 0.00mm (D3, on-pad), 1.17mm (D5), 1.92mm (D4), 4.41mm (D2) away, and every VSS pad sits solid in the F.Cu GND pour, so each supply loop is a few nH and current comes off the plane, not down a trace to a cap. At WS2812 edge rates with ~20mA per-channel steps that is single-digit mV droop, a local 100nF buys nothing measurable. Two paper arguments were wrong: distance to C2 (22-39mm) never mattered because C2 was not in the current path, and a cap cannot help the under-spec VDD item under High, 3.3V against a 3.7V minimum is a DC shortfall and decoupling only supplies transients. Plane-pair capacitance is not the mechanism either, ~2500mm2 over 1.6mm FR4 is only ~60pF. C2 removal stranded nothing: schematic `power:+3.3V` at 123.19,151.13 still feeds D2 pin 4 through the junction at 123.19,153.67 and `power:GND` at 152.4,165.1 feeds pin 2; board kept both `+3.3V` F.Cu tracks, they meet at 42.975,85.0 and carry D2 supply to the via at 43.0,86.25. ERC 0, DRC 0 errors 0 unconnected, unchanged. If bring-up shows LED trouble at 3.3V, fix the rail (VBUS or a boost), not the decoupling.
