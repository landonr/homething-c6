- [x] **MK1 ICS-43434 pin numbering fault (root cause of the dead microphone).** Found 2026-07-27 on the first hardware exercise of the mic; the full bring-up log was `docs/mic-bringup.md`, deleted 2026-07-31 and recoverable at `7b3563c`. MK1 reused the `inmp441_microphone_i2s:INMP441_Microphone_I2S` symbol, whose pin numbers are the INMP441's (1 SCK, 2 WS, 3 L/R, 4 SD, 5 VDD, 6 GND) and not the ICS-43434's (1 WS, 2 LR, 3 GND, 4 SCK, 5 VDD, 6 SD, per DS-000069 v1.2 Table 8). On the fabbed board that put the 3.072MHz bit clock on the mic WS pin, the 48kHz frame clock on LR, left the real SCK pin floating on the undriven `sd` net, and soldered the SD output pad into the GND pour. Symptom was 100% zero samples at the correct 192000 B/s byte rate on both `channel:` settings, because a mic clocked under 200kHz sits in standby with SD tristated while the ESP samples its own high-Z clock input. Nets and footprint were both innocent: the stock `Sensor_Audio:InvenSense_ICS-43434-6_3.5x2.65mm` has pad N equal to physical pin N, checked against datasheet Fig 3 and Fig 15. Third instance of this class after the ENC1 pad 6/8 swap and the SK6812 symbol/footprint pairing, so the `AGENTS.md` rule about checking pad numbering against the datasheet before adopting a part now has three scalps, and the specific trap here is reusing a symbol named for a different part in the same family. Resolved on schematic and board 2026-07-27 at b2d85c0: pins renumbered in both the embedded copy and the project-local `inmp441_microphone_i2s.kicad_sym`, positions and electrical types untouched so every wire endpoint and net-to-function pairing survived; board resynced with MK1 rotated 180 to 0 in place and the mic cluster rerouted, pads now `ws` on 1, GND on 2 and 3, `sck` on 4, `+3.3V` on 5, `sd` on 6, tracks 289 to 319 segments. The reroute also swapped the two signals at the XIAO end, `ws` moving to GPIO0 and `sd` to GPIO21, which every firmware then contradicted until 634d967; left uncorrected that is worse than the original fault, since driving LRCLK on GPIO21 puts a push-pull ESP output on the mic's SD output pad instead of merely leaving the part in standby. Current wiring, which is what firmware builds against: `sck` MK1.4 to U1.3 GPIO2 (`i2s_bclk_pin`), `ws` MK1.1 to U1.1 GPIO0 (`i2s_lrclk_pin`), `sd` MK1.6 to U1.4 GPIO21 (`i2s_din_pin`), GND on MK1.2 and MK1.3, `+3.3V` on MK1.5, and MK1.2 L/R on GND keeps `channel: left` correct. Check firmware against the exported netlist, never against another firmware, which is how the swap survived a pin-by-pin review. ## High

- [x] **Gate the LED rail: D2-D5 burn ~2.0mA quiescent even when dark.** Raised 2026-07-27. The four `XL-2020RGBC-WS2812B` sit permanently on `+3.3V`, and a WS2812-class part keeps its controller die powered and listening whenever VDD is present, so writing all channels to zero only zeroes the PWM duty and does not reduce the standing draw. At 0.5mA typical IDD each on the XINGLIGHT datasheet that is ~2.0mA continuous, already flagged in the LED chain item below as the dominant idle load, and it swamps everything else on the board: the R7/R8 battery divider is ~21uA, the PCF8575 a few uA, and a sleeping ESP32-C6 tens of uA. Consequence for power work: deep sleep cannot beat ~2.0mA no matter how well it is implemented, so this is the binding constraint on battery life and it caps the value of the wake-on-nINT item under Medium. Fixed in the design 2026-07-28, not yet in hardware. Q2 is an `AO3401A` P-channel MOSFET in SOT-23 (Alpha & Omega, Digi-Key 1855773, LCSC `C15127` with 228k in stock), source on `+3.3V`, drain on the new net `led_vdd` feeding all four LED VDD pads, gate on the new net `led_en`. Rds(on) is about 60 milliohm at Vgs -2.5V, so 22mV of drop at the 60mA full-white peak. R10 is a 1M 0603 (Yageo `RC0603FR-071ML`) gate pull-up to `+3.3V`, which makes the rail default OFF: `GPIO18` boots as a floating input and R10 holds the gate at the source, so the LEDs are dark through boot and reset, at a cost of 3.3uA while the rail is on. `led_en` lands on U1 pad 11, `GPIO18`/D10, which was the last unconnected XIAO edge pad; `TP_GPIO18_D10_MOSI` shares that pad so `led_en` has a probe point, and `GPIO6` (pad 29) and `GPIO7` (pad 25) remain free. Kept high-side exactly as this item required, so all four LED VSS pads still sit at `zone_connect 2` in the F.Cu GND pour. The four `+3.3V` vias that used to drop each LED VDD pad into the B.Cu plane were removed, and `led_vdd` routes 39.3mm F.Cu plus 51.9mm B.Cu with 4 vias. Layout trap worth recording: the first routing attempt ran `led_vdd` diagonally across F.Cu past the wheel and severed the GND pour neck, orphaning D5's VSS pad in a 77mm2 island, which produced the only unconnected item DRC found. Moving that branch to B.Cu fixed it and left the GND pour healthier than before, 2 islands at 2364.9 and 735.9mm2 with all four LED VSS pads in the main one, while the B.Cu `+3.3V` plane absorbed every moat and is still a single island at 3348.5mm2. ERC 0, DRC 0 errors, 0 unconnected, 43 warnings unchanged from baseline, parity 13 `extra_footprint` for `TP_*`. Firmware side done 2026-07-29, in `c6remote.yaml` and `c6remote-test-common.yaml` (which `c6remote-test-v1.yaml` and `c6remote-test-v2.yaml` both include), because `GPIO17` must be low or high-impedance whenever the rail is down or DIN pushes current through the LED input clamp into the dead rail and partially powers the chain, defeating the point. Four pieces: the `LED Rail` switch is `platform: gpio` on `GPIO18` with `inverted: true` and `restore_mode: ALWAYS_OFF`, so the rail is off through boot; the light's `on_turn_on` raises the rail, waits 20ms and re-sends, since the first frame is written in the same loop tick as the trigger before `led_vdd` has risen at D2; its `on_turn_off` waits 100ms so the final all-black frame lands while the LEDs still have power, then drops the rail, after which the RMT leaves `GPIO17` idling low; and `on_shutdown` drops the rail and puts both pins back to inputs, `GPIO17` with a pulldown because the RMT driver can leave a pull-up on it that would sit on DIN, `GPIO18` floating so R10 holds Q2 off. Both turn-off paths are guarded on light state so a turn-on inside either delay window is not undone. Added the same day: an `on_turn_off` interlock on the switch itself, since it is exposed as a `config` entity and turning it off by hand with the light still on would otherwise leave the RMT writing frames into a dead rail indefinitely; it blanks the light first, and the resulting second `switch.turn_off` re-fires the trigger once and stops on the guard. `esphome config` passes on all three configs. One residual and accepted: the strip component writes one all-black frame at setup before any automation runs, so DIN sees roughly 96 bit-symbols of 0.4us highs while the rail is still down, microseconds of clamp current into a 1uF rail, not worth gating the component's own setup for. Payoff is still unmeasured, idle should drop from ~2.0mA to tens of uA, at which point the R7/R8 divider at 21uA becomes the largest static load; a measurement on a rev-B board is the only proof, so that stays the open verification.

- [x] **Gate the IR receiver rail: U2 burns 350uA continuous and sets the standby floor.** Raised 2026-08-04 off the power budget below. With the LED rail gated, `U2` is 86% of the deep-sleep current all by itself: the TSOP4136 draws ISD 0.35mA typ (0.45mA max) at Vs 3.3V in the dark, rising to 0.45mA typ under 40klx sunlight, and nothing switches it. Everything else in deep sleep sums to 58uA, so the board sits at ~0.41mA asleep instead of ~0.06mA, which is 82 days of standby on a 1000mAh cell rather than 575. Same fix shape as `Q2`: a second AO3401A high-side P-FET on the `+3.3V` feed into `R6`, gate pull-up to hold it off by default. Pin cost is the blocker to check first, all 11 XIAO edge pads and all 16 expander IO are used, but `GPIO6` (pad 29) and `GPIO7` (pad 25) are free on the module's bottom row and both are in the `GPIO0`-`GPIO7` LP range. The open question was whether IR receive is meant to be a deep-sleep wake source, since that would force `U2` to stay powered. It is not, and cannot be as wired: `IR REC` sits on `GPIO16`, and the C6 wakes only on `GPIO0`-`GPIO7`, so `U2` was drawing 350uA through deep sleep to listen for something that could not wake anything. That settled it. Done 2026-08-04. `Q3` is a second `AO3401A` (same part, footprint and sourcing as `Q2`), source on `+3.3V`, drain on the new net `ir_vdd`, gate on the new net `ir_en`; `R11` is a 1M 0603 gate pull-up so the rail is off by default. `R6`'s upper end moved from `+3.3V` to `ir_vdd`, so the gate sits upstream of the existing `R6`/`C3` filter and that filter stays with `U2` untouched. `ir_en` lands on `U1` pad 29, `GPIO6`, which was unconnected and carried a no-connect flag; `GPIO7` (pad 25) is now the only spare. Placement follows the gate, not the load: the whole `ir_en` net (`U1`/29, `Q3`/1, `R11`/2) is compact down by `U1` because it is a 1M high-impedance node whenever `GPIO6` is an input, while `ir_vdd` takes the ~110mm run up to `R6` since 350uA of DC over that length is about 0.5 ohm and 0.2mV. That is the opposite of where `Q2` sits relative to its load, deliberately: the LED rail needed `Q2` and `C4` local because it slams 0 to 60mA at 800kHz, whereas the IR rail is steady 350uA and already has its own local filter. ## Power budget

Datasheet arithmetic as of 2026-08-04, no bench measurement exists. Every figure below is a typical at 25 °C unless noted. `U1` figures are Seeed's for the module, so they already include its LDO and power-path quiescent.

Three loads are zero in the sleep column: `D2`-`D5` sit behind `Q2` on `led_vdd`, `U2` sits behind `Q3` on `ir_vdd` as of 2026-08-04, and `MK1` drops to sleep on its own whenever the I²S clock stops, which ESPHome does whenever nothing is streaming.

**Deep sleep: ~58 µA**, since `Q3` now takes `U2`'s 350 µA out of the sleep column. Before gating it was ~0.41 mA. The largest remaining static load is the R7/R8 divider at 21 µA, which sits on the battery and cannot be switched.

**Awake and Wi-Fi connected: ~31 mA**, or ~85 mA with power save off. Board silicon contributes 0.9 mA of that, under 3%, so the radio is the entire awake budget and no passive component choice moves it. Gating buys nothing while awake, and is not meant to: the `IR Rail` switch defaults on.

Battery life at 80% usable capacity. No cell is specified anywhere in the design, `J1` is only the connector, so 1000 mAh is an assumption to make the numbers concrete; scale linearly for any other capacity.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep sleep only | 58 µA | 575 days |
| Always connected | 31 mA | 26 hours |
| 30-day target | 1.11 mA | 3.4% awake, about 49 min/day |

For comparison, before `Q3` the floor was 0.41 mA, which was 82 days of standby and only 33 min/day awake against the same 30-day target.
